# PAC hearing — HMRC, 18 May 2026

## Summary

The Public Accounts Committee questioned HMRC's John-Paul Marks and Nicole Newbury on compliance return on investment, currently around £95 collected for every £1 spent. HMRC is expanding its compliance and debt workforce by 5,500 and 2,400 staff respectively, targeting £10 billion in additional tax revenue by 2029-30. Compliance yield is forecast to hit a record ~£50 billion this year, rising to £60 billion by the end of the spending review period. Most of the new headcount is going into small business compliance (60% of the tax gap), with only "hundreds" (not thousands) going to the large business directorate — HMRC could not give the Chair a precise figure on the spot. Following an NAO recommendation, HMRC will pilot an expanded co-operative compliance approach with large businesses via three test-and-learn schemes starting in September. HMRC expects the value-for-money ratio (the £95-per-£1 figure) to stay roughly stable even as absolute yield grows, since added headcount carries its own admin cost.
