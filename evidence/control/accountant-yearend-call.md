# File Note — Year-End Review Call

**Client:** Hartley Joinery Ltd
**Call date:** 14 March 2026
**Period covered:** Year ended 31 December 2025
**Present:** Priya Nair (accountant), Tom Hartley (director), Sarah Lomax (bookkeeper)

## Summary

Priya opened by confirming the year's figures against what Sarah had sent over in Xero. Turnover for the year came in at £85,000, up from around £70,000 the prior year, with the increase mostly down to two kitchen fit-outs completed in the autumn. On purchases, Halden Timber remains the main supplier; an invoice that printed as "Haldane Timber" was confirmed as the same company, so it will stay coded under Halden Timber in the notes.

The business remains under the VAT registration threshold, and both Priya and Tom agreed this is the right call for now given that most customers are homeowners who would feel a 20% price rise. On the stove installs specifically, Priya corrected her initial view during the call: the correct treatment is zero-rated, not exempt, since these qualify as energy-saving installations. Sarah will flag this on invoices going forward so the coding stays correct at source.

Dave was taken on as a subcontractor in March; his UTR will be needed for the return. A repair figure of £1,412.50 that had been sitting in a suspense code was identified as the van repair from October and will be recoded to motor expenses. Net profit for the year came in ahead of the budget discussed in the spring, despite a difficult year operationally — the workshop roof needed replacing and the van needed repair, both in the same year, though Tom noted the figures held up better than it felt at the time. Tom was also told that HMRC will be issuing him a new personal tax code following the dividend taken in November; this is expected in the post in the coming weeks and needs no action from the practice.

Two decisions were agreed for the file: the business will move from the accruals basis to the cash basis for accounting, given its current size, which should reduce the volume of adjustments Sarah has to make; and drawings will be carried over using the same treatment as last year, unchanged. The use-of-home allowance is still worth claiming and will go back into the schedule. Corporation tax will work out at an effective 20% once marginal relief is applied. Priya will send bank statements copied for the audit trail, and draft accounts are expected in around three weeks, which Tom noted lines up with when the bank wants updated figures.

One item was left open and unresolved: Tom asked whether the workshop roof cost counts as a repair or an improvement for tax purposes; Priya deferred this to be picked up separately but the call did not return to it. The AGM paperwork also shows a note of "31 June," which is not a valid date and is being treated as a typo pending confirmation from Sarah of the correct date.
